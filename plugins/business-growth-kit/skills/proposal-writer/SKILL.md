# Proposal Writer

Use this skill when the user asks to create, improve, or structure a commercial proposal, sales deck outline, statement of work draft, executive pitch, outreach email, or follow-up sequence.

## Workflow

1. Identify the commercial situation:
   - Buyer or account type
   - Industry and business model
   - Current pain or trigger event
   - Desired outcome
   - Product, service, or solution being proposed
   - Known budget, timeline, stakeholders, and constraints

2. If critical information is missing, make reasonable assumptions and label them briefly. Ask follow-up questions only when the proposal would be materially misleading without the answer.

3. Build the proposal around business value before features:
   - Problem and context
   - Target outcome
   - Recommended approach
   - Scope and deliverables
   - Timeline or phases
   - Success metrics
   - Commercial assumptions
   - Risks and mitigations
   - Next step

4. Keep the tone practical, executive-friendly, and specific. Avoid hype, vague transformation language, and claims that cannot be supported.

5. When useful, include:
   - A concise executive summary
   - A fuller proposal body
   - A short email version
   - A follow-up plan
   - Open assumptions

## Output Style

Prefer clear section headings and compact bullets. Make the content ready to paste into a document or email. If pricing is unknown, use placeholders rather than inventing exact numbers.

## Quality Bar

- Lead with the buyer's business problem, not the seller's features.
- Explain why the proposed approach fits this buyer.
- Include concrete next steps.
- Separate facts from assumptions.
- Avoid legal, financial, or contractual certainty unless the user provides approved wording.
